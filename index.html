    03 / TEDDY’S GLOVEBOX NOTEBOOK

    How did the corgi do that?

    For curious paws. Open a page after you play.

    Teddy has agreed to reveal his secrets. Gwinn has agreed to chew the paperwork. These are the actual tricks inside Stripe Chase, with real game files to inspect.

    The door that counts stripes

    The pieces add to a counter. The car door asks for that road’s quota, spends those pieces, and changes into an open door. Each road has its own counter, so yesterday’s stripes cannot persuade today’s door.

    Teddy’s lesson: count your blessings. Also your stripes. Doors are very literal.

    Look in scripts/chapters.ts and scripts/stripe-car.ts. Background blocks hold the pickup and door rules; the map places them.

    Maple Street blocks (.BBL)Maple Street map (.MAP)
    The leaf with its own traffic light

    Gwinn’s very important leaf gets a turn before Doc Leo. WAIT shows a solid striped barrier; GO shows an open path. The sign, barrier, and leaf all change on the engine’s clock. The pictures and collision rules change together.

    Teddy’s lesson: make room for little friends. Even the ones who weigh less than a sneeze.

    Look in scripts/leaf-crossing.ts. Animated background blocks create the timing challenge. No extra key, damage, or browser timer required.

    Downtown’s timed blocks (.BBL)Downtown map (.MAP)
    The stripes that took flying lessons

    Maple Street’s stripe kites are moving actors on a small looping path. Hop to catch them. Seven pieces stay on the ground, so just one kite can complete the quota. The wind posts send replacements if the first batch gets away.

    Teddy’s lesson: try again with a little patience. The wind packed seventeen million spare chances in a very small pocket.

    Look in scripts/wind-kites.ts. Native paths keep the kites above the sidewalk. Background tiles create new kites, and a lifetime limit keeps the sky from filling up forever.

    Kite flight paths (.MON)Flying stripe pictures (.MBL)
    The balloon dogs who understand “woof”

    The friendly balloons are Game-Maker monsters. Teddy’s bark is strong enough to turn one into a stripe piece; a casual bump is not. “Monster” is just the engine’s name for an actor. These ones have excellent manners. Their stripes are bonuses: the twelve visible course pieces already cover the ten-piece quota.

    Teddy’s lesson: sometimes asking nicely works better than hopping on somebody’s head.

    Look in scripts/chapters.ts. A monster’s power and replacement monster make the exchange happen.

    Balloon dog rules (.MON)Teddy’s real bark (.VOC)
    The sky with an upstairs

    The moving arrows are two animated tiles that both pull upward. Cloud ledges are solid only on top, so Teddy rises through them and lands above them. Collecting a stripe swaps its tile for matching sky. No floating wallpaper!

    Teddy’s lesson: pause, catch your breath, and let a little help lift you. The clouds have not been trained to fetch.

    Look in scripts/sky-laundry.ts. Gravity, animation, and one-sided walls build the crossing.

    Wind and cloud tiles (.BBL)Sky Laundry map (.MAP)
    The shortcut that forgot to be short

    Gwinn takes the controls in her garden maze, with her own pictures and a four-way character file. Gravity is switched off in its scenery, and walking frames move in four directions. The pawprints point toward the exit; side nooks hold spare stripes. Every path can be retraced.

    Teddy’s lesson: patience helps a friend find the way. Especially when “one more turn” has eight turns inside it.

    Look in scripts/gwinn-maze.ts. This chapter uses the same engine with Gwinn’s own pictures. The native character supplies the new moves.

    Four-way Gwinn (.CHR)Gwinn’s garden maze (.MAP)
    The crew who share the controls

    Teddy leads the chase, then appoints Doc Leo Chief Leaf Supervisor Downtown. Doc Leo has a two-block character, taller crate hops, and a rubber-chicken squeak. Gwinn takes over the four-way maze. Each scene chooses its own native character and picture files.

    Teddy’s lesson: a hero makes room for friends. The chicken has requested a director’s chair.

    Look in scripts/crew-characters.ts. Walking jumps use one momentum impulse per press; holding Up does not fly.

    Doc Leo’s moves (.CHR)Gwinn’s pictures (.CBL)
    The foam that refuses to be a pitfall

    The Clown Club’s stepping stones sit over a soft foam tray. Missing a jump costs no try: arrows at either end lift Teddy back to the course. The challenge is landing on the stones, with room to recover and try again.

    Teddy’s lesson: mistakes are part of learning. Occasionally they are also full of foam.

    Look in scripts/course-obstacles.ts. Solid tiles make the stepping stones; upward gravity builds the return lifts.

    Foam and stepping stones (.BBL)The Clown Club course (.MAP)
    The car that is secretly a character

    On the victory lap, THE CHEZ takes Teddy’s place as the playable character. Steering adds momentum. Letting go lets it fade. Bouncing adds a little upward nudge, and honking leaves the wheels rolling. Teddy supervises from the passenger window.

    Teddy’s lesson: a good teammate gives directions. A great teammate also says “meep.”

    Look in scripts/victory-lap.ts. The character file stores the moves; the character blocks hold the pictures.

    CitiCar moves (.CHR)CitiCar pictures (.CBL)
    The demonstration with invisible paws

    “Watch Teddy” plays a tiny recording of keys and their timing through the same engine you play. The recording contains no video. The engine walks Teddy, gathers the pieces, and drives home all over again.

    Teddy’s lesson: practice makes progress. Replaying your best woof is also acceptable.

    Look in src/main.ts for playback and scripts/check-game.ts for the replay check. A changed road may need a new recording.

    Teddy’s complete key recording (.REC)Try “Watch Teddy”
    The music and the very dramatic cartoons

    Eight arrangements share an original stripe-chasing tune. The engine plays their OPL2 instruments from CMF music files. Seven road cartoons connect Teddy’s adventures, from a suspicious leaf to its thank-you note. Intro, road trips, victory, and the tiny-nap ending use FLI animations. Doc Leo’s 900% claim remains 100% theatrical.

    Teddy’s lesson: share the spotlight with your friends. Keep the theme tune.

    Look in scripts/music.ts, scripts/cartoons.ts, and scripts/road-stories.ts. These files generate the notes, jokes, and animation frames.

    Victory lap music (.CMF)Opening cartoon (.FLI)

    The downloads are original-format game data, meant for inspecting with Game-Maker tools. To run them, keep the complete game folder together. The project repository contains the builders and the full public/game/ folder. All eight chapters run in the @lewismoten/gamemaker-sandbox-engine package.
