    03 / TEDDY’S LAB NOTEBOOK

    How does the speedometer work?

    Open a page after you play.

    These are the actual tricks inside The Stripe Hypothesis, with real game files to inspect.

    The speedometer that is part of the car

    Each speed is its own native character file. Pressing Right moves the car a fixed number of pixels every tick: 4, 5 or 6 for 20, 25 or 30 mph. The speedometer is the top block of that same character, so its number can never disagree with the car. A scene link on a hilltop swaps in the next speed’s car.

    Teddy’s lesson: a number you can’t change is a number you can trust.

    Look in scripts/hypo-car.ts and scripts/check-speed.ts

    The 25 mph car (.CHR)Car and speedometer pictures (.CBL)
    The stripes are a palette swap

    The car is one picture. Its stripes use three palette slots, so each trial’s palette turns them from bare red to one stripe, both stripes and finally the white pinstripe, without redrawing anything.

    Teddy’s lesson: change the paint, not the car.

    Look in scripts/hypo-art.ts

    Control Run’s palette (.PAL)Car pictures (.CBL)
    The turtle with a countdown

    The barrier, the turtle and the countdown sign are twelve block variants that change together every nine ticks: six of WAIT with the bar draining, then six of GO. Only the closed ones are solid, so holding Right is safe and waiting wins.

    Teddy’s lesson: some things just take their time.

    Look in scripts/hypo-street.ts

    Timed blocks (.BBL)Control Run map (.MAP)
    Teddy, four ways

    On foot in the driveway Teddy faces up, down, left and right. The side pose is mirrored by the frame, and Space barks with his real bark. The garage door counts the clipboard and the stopwatch, then opens.

    Teddy’s lesson: always check behind the hedge.

    Look in scripts/hypo-driveway.ts

    Four-way Teddy (.CHR)The driveway (.MAP)
    The demonstration with invisible paws

    “Watch Teddy” replays a recording of keys and their timing through the same engine you play. A headless script plays the game with arrows only and saves the recording.

    Teddy’s lesson: slow, steady hands can win.

    Look in scripts/record-demo.ts

    The key recording (.REC)

    The downloads are original-format game data, meant for inspecting with Game-Maker tools. To run them, keep the complete game folder together. The project repository contains the builders and the full public/game/ folder. Both levels run in the @lewismoten/gamemaker-sandbox-engine package.
